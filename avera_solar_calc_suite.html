Irradiance Note PSH

Peak sun hours and daily irradiation are numerically identical: 1 PSH = 1 kWh/m²/day (1,000 W/m² for one hour). Peninsular Malaysia typically sees ~3.4–4.0 PSH depending on location and season. Set your project value in Settings.

Sizing & Energy Terms

Peak Sun Hours PSH
The number of hours per day the sun would need to shine at 1,000 W/m² to deliver the site's actual total daily irradiation. Numerically identical to kWh/m²/day. Peninsular Malaysia typically runs ~3.4–4.0 PSH.
Performance Ratio PR
The fraction of theoretical energy (kWp × PSH) an array actually delivers after real-world losses: soiling, temperature, wiring, inverter efficiency, shading. 0.75–0.85 is typical for a well-designed system.
kWp
Kilowatt-peak: the array's rated DC output under Standard Test Conditions (STC). This is the "size" quoted for modules and most system capacities.
Duty factor / operating days
The share of the week a site actually runs (e.g. 5/7 for a site closed on weekends). Under self-consumption with no battery, generation on non-operating days is wasted, so fewer operating days per week means the array needs to work harder on the days it does run to hit the same monthly target.
Daytime consumption share
Of the total monthly bill, the estimated share that actually happens during sun hours (roughly 8am–6pm). Consumption outside that window can never be solar-offset regardless of system size or battery.
Offset target
How much of the solar-reachable load you're deliberately sizing the array to chase. Below 100% builds in a safety margin against an optimistic Daytime Share assumption; used cautiously for PPA to avoid oversizing.
Extended closures
Any multi-week period a site is shut, whatever the reason: school term breaks, factory shutdowns, festival closures (CNY/Raya), annual maintenance. Doesn't change the required array size, but reduces how much of the year's generation actually gets billed under a no-battery, no-netting scheme.

Electrical Terms

Voc
Open-circuit voltage: the voltage a panel or string produces with no current flowing. Rises as temperature drops below the 25°C STC reference, which is why cold-morning Voc is checked against the inverter's max DC input.
DC/AC ratio
Array DC capacity (kWp) divided by inverter AC rating (kW). Deliberately oversizing the DC side (typically 1.1–1.3×) captures more low-light and off-peak-sun generation; too high causes clipping at midday.
kWp vs kWac
kWp is DC array capacity; kWac is the inverter's AC output capacity. NEM/SELCO quotas and TNB applications are usually stated in kWac, while modules are quoted in kWp; the two are only equal at a DC/AC ratio of 1.0.
Depth of Discharge DoD
The share of a battery's nominal capacity that's usable before it's considered "empty." A 90% DoD on a 10 kWh battery gives 9 kWh of usable energy; discharging further shortens battery life.

Financial & Schemes Terms

Solar ATAP
A Malaysian rooftop solar scheme (commonly used for schools/government buildings) that is self-consumption only: there is no export credit or netting. Any generation the site can't use in real time is simply lost.
NEM / NOVA
Net Energy Metering: a scheme where excess exported generation nets against consumption elsewhere in the billing period, so timing mismatches between generation and load matter far less than under ATAP.
Curtailment
The share of an array's potential output that's generated but never billed: because there's no load to consume it (non-operating days) and no way to store or export it (no battery, no netting).
PPA
Power Purchase Agreement: zero CAPEX to the client; Avera owns and finances the system and is paid per kWh actually consumed, typically over ~21 years. Under this model, curtailed generation is Avera's stranded capital, not the client's cost.
Simple payback
Total system cost ÷ annual savings or revenue. Ignores degradation, tariff inflation and O&M; a quick first-pass figure only, not a full lifecycle financial model.

Regulatory Bodies Malaysia

TNB
Tenaga Nasional Berhad: the national electricity utility; owns the grid and issues connection approvals.
SEDA
Sustainable Energy Development Authority: administers Malaysia's renewable energy schemes and incentives.
Suruhanjaya Tenaga
The Energy Commission: the regulator for electricity supply and licensing in Peninsular Malaysia and Sabah.
